decided by where you studied, not your citizenship [X]. That

3. THE FLOW - applying to a research program end to end
================================================================

This is the supervisor-driven research route (Mitacs GRA, ELAP
research placements, most direct lab outreach). The Mitacs GRI
is the exception - it is portal-driven and you pick from a
posted project list rather than courting a supervisor. Where
they diverge is marked.

Total realistic lead time: 9 to 12 months from first email to
landing in Canada. [~]


---- PHASE 0: ELIGIBILITY TRIAGE (1 evening) ----

Before anything else, kill the programs you cannot use. Most
wasted application effort dies at a gate the applicant could
have checked on day one.

The four gates that actually disqualify people:

  1. Enrolment status. GRI needs current full-time undergrad
     enrolment [XX]. GRA needs senior-undergrad, grad or
     postdoc enrolment [XX]. ELAP needs enrolment at a Latin
     American or Caribbean institution you will return to [XX].
     A conferred degree with no current enrolment fails all
     three.

  2. Institution-level eligibility, not country-level. Mitacs
     publishes eligible INSTITUTIONS per partner country. Peru
     appears on the 2027 country list [X], but your specific
     university must also be listed. This is the most common
     silent rejection - the country list gets quoted in blog
     posts, the institution list does not.

  3. Semesters remaining. GRI reportedly requires one to three
     semesters still remaining as of fall 2027 [X]. Graduating
     too early disqualifies you as surely as graduating too
     late.

  4. Which deadline tier. McCall MacBain has two, four weeks
     apart, decided by where you studied [X].

TIP: Write these four answers down before you read another
program page. If you cannot answer gate 2 from the official
list, email the program directly and ask. A one-line answer
from Mitacs beats a week of guessing.


---- PHASE 1: TARGET SELECTION (2-3 weeks) ----

PORTAL ROUTE (Mitacs GRI):
  You browse posted projects on globalink.mitacs.ca and submit
  a shortlist. Reported requirement: at least 3 and no more
  than 10 projects, drawn from at least 3 different Canadian
  provinces [X].

  The province constraint is not bureaucratic noise - it forces
  geographic spread so the matching algorithm has room. Do not
  submit ten projects at three Toronto labs.

  TIP: Rank honestly. A project you would decline if offered is
  a slot you wasted. [~]

SUPERVISOR ROUTE (GRA, ELAP research, direct outreach):
  You find the lab yourself. Sequence that works:

  a. Start from papers, not from university rankings. Find 3-4
     recent papers you can actually read and care about. The
     corresponding author is your target.
  b. Check they are still at that institution and still active
     - a 2023 paper does not mean a 2026 lab.
  c. Check the lab page for an explicit "not taking students"
     notice before you write. Ignoring one is an instant no.
  d. Build a list of 15-20. Expect a reply rate around 10-20%
     for a cold, well-targeted email. [~]

  TIP: Overlap beats prestige. A supervisor at a mid-ranked
  university whose exact subfield matches yours will say yes
  far more often than a famous lab where you are one of 200
  identical emails. [~]


---- PHASE 2: THE SUPERVISOR EMAIL (the real bottleneck) ----

Applies to GRA and ELAP. Skip if you are on the GRI portal
route.

This is where the process actually succeeds or fails. The
Mitacs GRA has no open portal - without a committed host
supervisor there is nothing to submit [XX]. Your route in is
a person, not a form.

Structure that works [~]:

  Subject: concrete and specific. "Prospective Mitacs Globalink
  intern - [your subfield]" beats "Research opportunity
  inquiry".

  Paragraph 1 (2 sentences): who you are, what you study, when
  you are available.

  Paragraph 2 (3-4 sentences): the specific paper of theirs you
  read, and one concrete thought about it - an extension, a
  question, a dataset it could be applied to. This paragraph is
  the entire email. Everything else is packaging.

  Paragraph 3 (2 sentences): what you bring. Be concrete -
  "I have implemented X in PyTorch and shipped Y" beats
  "I am passionate about research".

  Paragraph 4 (1 sentence): the funding. Say the program name
  and that it funds the placement at no cost to their grant.
  This is the sentence that converts. A supervisor who learns
  the student arrives pre-funded reads the rest of the email
  differently.

  Attach: a one-page CV. Nothing else.

  Total: under 200 words.

TIPS:
  - Send Tuesday to Thursday morning in THEIR timezone. [~]
  - One follow-up after 10-14 days. One. Then move on. [~]
  - Do not mass-BCC. Academics talk, and a generic email is
    visible from orbit.
  - If they say "I have no funding", reply immediately with
    the program details. Half of these reversals happen right
    there. [~]


---- PHASE 3: DOCUMENTS (2-4 weeks, overlaps phase 2) ----

Standard set for research funding applications [~]:

  - Academic CV, 1-2 pages. Research, publications, technical
    skills first. Retail jobs last or absent.
  - Official transcript. Ordering this takes longer than you
    think - start in week one, not the week before.
  - Statement of purpose / research statement. Usually
    500-1000 words.
  - 2-3 reference letters. Give referees 3-4 weeks minimum,
    plus your CV, the program description, and a bullet list
    of what you did with them. A referee who has to reconstruct
    your contribution writes a weak letter.
  - Language proof. IELTS/TOEFL for English, sometimes TEF/TCF
    for French Quebec institutions. Test slots book out weeks
    ahead. [~]
  - Passport copy, valid well past the placement end date.

TIP: The statement of purpose is where most applications go
flat. The failure mode is describing your enthusiasm instead
of your fit. Write it so the specific supervisor or committee
sees the overlap in the first three sentences. If your SOP
would work unchanged for a different lab, it is too generic. [~]

TIP: Start the transcript and reference requests FIRST, before
writing anything. They are the only items whose timeline you
do not control. [~]


---- PHASE 4: SUBMISSION ----

GRI: submit through the Globalink portal before the deadline.
16 September 2026, 1:00 p.m. Pacific for the 2027 cohort [XX].
Note the time zone - 1:00 p.m. Pacific is 3:00 p.m. in Lima,
same day.

GRA: your host supervisor or their graduate office drives the
submission [XX]. Ask them for the internal deadline the moment
they agree - institution deadlines sit weeks before any
external one.

ELAP: apply through whichever channel exists - your home
university's international office, or the Canadian host
institution's own inbound call [XX]. Nothing sent directly to
Global Affairs Canada will be read under the 2026-27 model.

McCall MacBain: apply yourself at
apply.mccallmacbainscholars.org [XX]. Separately, you must meet
McGill admission requirements for an eligible master's or
professional programme [XX]. Two processes, both required.

TIP: Submit 48 hours early. Portal load spikes on deadline day
and no program treats a failed upload as your problem. [~]


---- PHASE 5: WAITING AND MATCHING (2-5 months) ----

For GRI, a September application feeds a summer placement the
following year - roughly a 9-month gap [~]. Results and
matching land in between.

What actually happens in this window [~]:
  - Applications are screened for eligibility. Gate failures
    are cut here without review.
  - Surviving applications go to the supervisors whose projects
    you ranked.
  - A supervisor expresses interest, and a match is made.
  - An offer or award letter issues, which is where the exact
    funding amount is confirmed - Mitacs does not publish one
    fixed stipend figure, the letter states it [X].

TIP: Silence in month three is normal, not a rejection. Do not
email the program office for status. Do keep the supervisor
warm if you have one - a short note with a relevant paper is
welcome; a "any news?" is not. [~]

TIP: Apply to more than one thing in the same season. These
timelines are long enough that a single application is a
9-month bet on one outcome. [~]


---- PHASE 6: ACCEPTED - THE LOGISTICS PHASE ----

This is the phase the ELAP winner interview covers, and the
phase I could not extract for you. Treat everything below as
the checklist to VERIFY against that video and the official
guidance, not as confirmed fact.

IMMIGRATION [!!]
  A short research placement in Canada may require a study
  permit, a work permit, or fall under an exemption depending
  on length, whether you are paid, and whether the work is
  academic. I could not verify which applies to a 12-week
  Mitacs placement in 2027, and getting this wrong is a
  boarding-denied error, not a paperwork error.

  ASK THE PROGRAM DIRECTLY, in writing, the moment you are
  accepted. Then confirm against IRCC. Do not rely on a
  YouTube video from 2022 for immigration guidance - this is
  the single most likely thing in this whole file to have
  changed.

  Whatever the answer, processing times for Peruvian applicants
  can run months. Start the day the offer lands.

MONEY TIMING [~]
  The gap that catches people: flights and first month's rent
  are due BEFORE the first stipend payment arrives. Mitacs
  funds airfare, housing, food and incidentals [X], but
  reimbursement timing is a separate question from coverage.

  Ask explicitly: is airfare booked for me, reimbursed after I
  fly, or advanced? The answer changes whether you need roughly
  CAD 2,000-3,000 of bridge money.

  Enrolment fee support is reportedly capped at CAD 300 [X].
  Anything above that is yours.

HOUSING [~]
  Canadian student housing markets are tight in Toronto,
  Vancouver and Montreal, and a summer placement puts you in
  the worst possible window.

  - Ask the host university's international office about
    on-campus summer residence FIRST. It is usually cheaper,
    furnished, and requires no local credit history.
  - Off campus, landlords may ask for Canadian credit history
    or guarantors you do not have. Student residences and
    university-affiliated housing sidestep this.
  - Mitacs reportedly provides student mentors on campus [X].
    Use them - a mentor who already lives there is worth more
    than a week of listings research.
  - Never wire a deposit for a place you have not had someone
    physically verify.

INSURANCE [X]
  Mitacs reportedly provides emergency health insurance for
  the internship period. Confirm what it excludes - emergency
  cover is not the same as health cover.

TIP: The highest-value thing in that ELAP interview is likely
the flight and housing sequencing - what they booked, when,
and what they wish they had done first. Extract it with the
prompt in section 7 and paste the result under this section. [~]


---- PHASE 7: ARRIVAL AND FIRST WEEKS ----

  - Ask your supervisor for a first-week goal before you fly.
    Arriving with a defined first task compresses the useless
    orientation period from three weeks to three days. [~]
  - Set up a local bank account and phone plan in week one.
    Many stipend disbursements need a Canadian account. [~]
  - Meet the lab's grad students, not just the PI. They decide
    whether you get real work or busy work. [~]


---- PHASE 8: CONVERTING IT INTO SOMETHING ----

A 12-week placement is short. What makes it compound [~]:

  - Ask in week two what a publishable contribution would look
    like. Do not wait until week ten.
  - Get the reference letter conversation started at week
    eight, while the work is fresh and you are still in the
    building.
  - The Mitacs Globalink pathway is explicitly designed to feed
    graduate study in Canada. If you want to return for a
    master's, say so to your supervisor before you leave. A
    supervisor who knows you want to come back writes a
    different letter than one who thinks you are done. [~]
