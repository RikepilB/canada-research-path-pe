The flow — application to arrival

This is the supervisor-driven research route (Mitacs GRA, ELAP research placements, direct lab outreach). The Mitacs internship is the exception — it is portal-driven, you pick from a posted project list rather than courting a supervisor. Divergences are marked. Realistic lead time from first email to landing in Canada: 9 to 12 months.

Phase 0 Eligibility triage one evening

Kill the programs you cannot use before you write a word. Most wasted effort dies at a gate the applicant could have checked on day one. The four that actually disqualify people:

  1. Enrolment status. The internship needs current full-time undergrad enrolment; the Research Award needs senior-undergrad, grad or postdoc enrolment; ELAP needs enrolment at a Latin American or Caribbean institution you will return to. A conferred degree with no current enrolment fails all three.
  2. Institution-level eligibility, not country-level. Peru appears on the Mitacs 2027 country list, but your specific university must also be on the eligible-institution list. This is the most common silent rejection — the country list gets quoted in blog posts, the institution list does not.
  3. Semesters remaining. The internship reportedly requires one to three semesters still remaining as of fall 2027. Graduating too early disqualifies you as surely as graduating too late.
  4. Which deadline tier. McCall MacBain has two, four weeks apart, decided by where you studied.

Write these four answers down before reading another program page. If you cannot answer #2 from the official list, email Mitacs and ask. A one-line reply beats a week of guessing.

Phase 1 Target selection 2–3 weeks

Portal route (Mitacs internship). Browse posted projects and submit a shortlist — reportedly at least 3 and no more than 10, drawn from at least 3 different provinces. The province constraint is not bureaucratic noise; it forces geographic spread so matching has room. Do not submit ten projects at three Toronto labs. Rank honestly — a project you would decline if offered is a slot you wasted.

Supervisor route (Research Award, ELAP research, direct outreach). You find the lab yourself:

  • Start from papers, not rankings. Find 3–4 recent papers you can actually read and care about. The corresponding author is your target.
  • Check they are still at that institution and still active — a 2023 paper does not mean a 2026 lab.
  • Check the lab page for an explicit "not taking students" notice before writing. Ignoring one is an instant no.
  • Build a list of 15–20. Expect roughly a 10–20% reply rate on cold, well-targeted email.

Overlap beats prestige. A mid-ranked supervisor whose exact subfield matches yours says yes far more often than a famous lab where you are one of 200 identical emails.

Phase 2 The supervisor email the real bottleneck

Applies to the Research Award and ELAP; skip it on the internship portal route. The Research Award has no open portal — without a committed host supervisor there is nothing to submit. Your route in is a person, not a form.

Under 200 words, four paragraphs:

  • Subject line — concrete. "Prospective Mitacs Globalink intern — [your subfield]" beats "Research opportunity inquiry".
  • ¶1, two sentences — who you are, what you study, when you are available.
  • ¶2, three or four sentences — the specific paper of theirs you read and one concrete thought about it: an extension, a question, a dataset it could apply to. This paragraph is the entire email. Everything else is packaging.
  • ¶3, two sentences — what you bring, concretely. "I implemented X in PyTorch and shipped Y" beats "I am passionate about research".
  • ¶4, one sentence — the funding. Name the program and say it funds the placement at no cost to their grant. This is the sentence that converts — a supervisor who learns the student arrives pre-funded reads the rest of the email differently.
  • Attach — a one-page CV. Nothing else.

Send Tuesday–Thursday morning in their timezone. One follow-up after 10–14 days, then move on. Never mass-BCC — academics talk, and a generic email is visible from orbit. If they reply "I have no funding", send the program details immediately; half of those reversals happen right there.

Phase 3 Documents 2–4 weeks, overlaps phase 2
  • Academic CV, 1–2 pages. Research, publications and technical skills first; retail jobs last or absent.
  • Official transcript. Ordering takes longer than you think — start week one.
  • Statement of purpose, usually 500–1000 words.
  • Two or three reference letters. Give referees 3–4 weeks minimum, plus your CV, the program description, and a bullet list of what you did with them. A referee reconstructing your contribution writes a weak letter.
  • Language proof — IELTS/TOEFL, sometimes TEF/TCF for francophone Quebec institutions. Test slots book out weeks ahead.
  • Passport valid well past the placement end date.

Start transcripts and reference requests first, before writing anything. They are the only items whose timeline you do not control. And the statement of purpose is where most applications go flat: the failure mode is describing your enthusiasm instead of your fit. If your SOP would work unchanged for a different lab, it is too generic.

Phase 4 Submission
  • Mitacs internship — submit through the Globalink portal by 16 September 2026, 1:00 p.m. Pacific. That is 3:00 p.m. in Lima the same day.
  • Research Award — your host supervisor or their graduate office drives the submission. Ask for the internal deadline the moment they agree; institution deadlines sit weeks ahead of external ones.
  • ELAP — apply through your home university's international office or the Canadian host institution's own inbound call. Nothing sent directly to Global Affairs Canada is read under the 2026–27 model.
  • McCall MacBain — apply yourself, and separately meet McGill's admission requirements for an eligible programme. Two processes, both required.

Submit 48 hours early. Portals spike on deadline day and no program treats a failed upload as its problem.

Phase 5 Waiting and matching 2–5 months

A September application feeds a placement the following summer — roughly a nine-month gap. In between: applications are screened for eligibility (gate failures are cut here without review), survivors go to the supervisors whose projects you ranked, a supervisor expresses interest, a match is made, and an award letter issues. That letter is where the exact funding figure is confirmed — Mitacs does not publish one fixed stipend.

Silence in month three is normal, not a rejection. Do not email the program office for status. Do keep a supervisor warm if you have one — a short note with a relevant paper is welcome; "any news?" is not. And apply to more than one thing per season: these timelines are long enough that a single application is a nine-month bet on one outcome.

Phase 6 Accepted — logistics the expensive phase
This is the phase the ELAP winner interview covers, and the phase that could not be extracted — YouTube was blocked when this page was built. Treat everything below as the checklist to verify against that video and official guidance, not as confirmed fact.

Immigration. A short research placement may require a study permit, a work permit, or fall under an exemption depending on length, whether you are paid, and whether the work is academic. Which applies to a 12-week 2027 Mitacs placement is unverified here, and getting it wrong is a boarding-denied error, not a paperwork error. Ask the program in writing the day you are accepted, then confirm against IRCC. Do not take immigration guidance from a 2022 video — this is the single most likely thing on this page to have changed.

Money timing. The gap that catches people: flights and first month's rent fall due before the first stipend arrives. Coverage and reimbursement timing are different questions. Ask explicitly whether airfare is booked for you, reimbursed after you fly, or advanced — the answer decides whether you need roughly CAD 2,000–3,000 of bridge money. Enrolment-fee support is reportedly capped at CAD 300; anything above that is yours.

Housing. Toronto, Vancouver and Montreal are tight, and a summer placement hits the worst window. Ask the host university's international office about on-campus summer residence first — usually cheaper, furnished, and no local credit history required. Off campus, landlords may want Canadian credit history or guarantors you do not have. Mitacs reportedly provides campus student mentors; a mentor who already lives there is worth more than a week of listings research. Never wire a deposit for a place nobody has physically verified.

Insurance. Emergency health insurance is reportedly provided for the internship period. Confirm what it excludes — emergency cover is not health cover.

Phase 7 Arrival and first weeks
  • Ask your supervisor for a first-week goal before you fly. Arriving with a defined first task compresses the useless orientation period from three weeks to three days.
  • Bank account and phone plan in week one — many stipend disbursements need a Canadian account.
  • Meet the lab's grad students, not just the PI. They decide whether you get real work or busy work.
Phase 8 Converting it into something

Twelve weeks is short. What makes it compound:

  • Ask in week two what a publishable contribution would look like. Do not wait until week ten.
  • Start the reference-letter conversation at week eight, while the work is fresh and you are still in the building.
  • The Globalink pathway is explicitly designed to feed graduate study in Canada. If you want to come back for a master's, say so before you leave — a supervisor who knows that writes a different letter than one who thinks you are done.

Extraction prompt for the videos

First, a verbatim transcript with speaker labels and rough timestamps every 30 seconds.

Second, an extraction table with these columns: program name, funding body, who is eligible, award value in CAD, duration, deadline mentioned, whether the applicant applies directly or must be nominated, whether a host supervisor is required first, and required documents.

Pull only what the speaker actually says. For anything implied but not stated, mark it "not stated" rather than inferring.

At the end, list every date, dollar figure, and URL mentioned, and flag which claims sound like they could be out of date given the video's upload year.

Do not add advice or context from your own knowledge; this is an extraction task.

The "do not add context" line is the load-bearing part. If you merge six videos into one dataset, you want the model's inferences kept out of the extraction layer so conflicts stay visible instead of being silently smoothed over.
